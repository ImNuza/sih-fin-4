# ILAS: "Parametric Insurance – A Useful Tool for Fighting the Climate Emergency?" (summary)

- Source: https://www.ilas.sg/insurance-law-knowledge/parametricinsuranceandclimate
- Publisher: Insurance Law Association of Singapore (ILAS). Author: Donald Spencer. Dated "May 22" (year not shown on the page).
- This is a summary for team use, not a copy. Read the original for exact wording.

## Main points

**What parametric insurance is.** It pays a fixed, pre-agreed sum when a measured index (rainfall, wind speed, hail size, cloud cover) crosses a threshold. The payout depends on the size of the event, not on the actual damage. Flight-delay cover in Singapore is the familiar local example: payment is automatic once flight data confirms the delay.

**Advantages.** Speed (cash right after the event, no loss assessment). Transparency (the trigger and amount are known in advance). It can fill gaps where indemnity cover is too costly or not available, if good data exists.

**Main weakness: basis risk.** The index does not match the real loss exactly. The article names both sides: under-compensation and over-compensation. It also depends on data quality and on data sets staying available (it cites NOAA stopping updates to a disasters database).

**Real examples.** Caribbean CCRIF (multi-country pool for governments). ACRE in East Africa (smallholder farmers, paid by mobile money when weather stations detect stress). African Risk Capacity flood product (2023). A WTW and BHP reef policy in Fiji. Most are sold to governments, aid groups and NGOs, not to individuals.

## The Singapore legal question (most useful part for us)

- Singapore has no statutory definition of a contract of insurance. Courts use an old English test: a payment due on an uncertain event that is adverse to the insured person. The author thinks parametric policies probably meet it.
- **Insurance Act 1966, section 151(1):** insurance with no interest in the event, or by way of gaming or wagering, is void. **Section 151(3):** you cannot recover more than the value of your interest. **Section 151(4)** carves out contracts of indemnity.
- So a parametric policy is safe in either of two ways:
  1. It counts as an indemnity, because the payout is a genuine pre-estimate of the likely loss (like a "valued policy").
  2. Or the buyer has an insurable interest: they really suffer an economic loss when the event happens.
- If neither holds, the policy risks being void as a wager (Insurance Act s151(1) or Civil Law Act s5).
- In Nov 2024 DPM Gan Kim Yong said MAS is studying ways to support parametric insurance. In 2020 the SAL Law Reform Committee recommended removing the insurable-interest requirement for non-life and indemnity policies.
- Outlook: expect hybrid products, with parametric parts added to normal indemnity policies to give fast cash after a disaster.

## What this means for our product

- This is the legal reason behind the brief's "insurable interest" rule. Sell only to people who lose when the trigger fires, and **size the payout as a pre-estimate of their loss** (for example, a day's lost income or lost wages for rest breaks). Then it is insurance, not a bet.
- A **hybrid** design (fast parametric payout plus normal cover) matches where the article says the market is going, and the brief lists "parametric or hybrid products" as in scope.
- Our pitch should say this in one line: why the product is insurance under section 151 and not a wager.
