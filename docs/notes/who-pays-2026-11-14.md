# Who pays the premium (14 Nov 2026)

Notion todo: decide who pays the premium for each plan: member, platform, employer, union, or a time-limited subsidy shown on the premium line with an end date. The brief says "Decide who pays before designing" and "No permanent subsidy" (`../problem-statements.md`, Constraints).

## Where we are

- **Bhoomi's SafeDay design doc** (Notion, section "Decide who pays first") proposes a worker plan of S$4.40 a week:
  - the worker pays S$1.50
  - the platform pays S$1.50
  - a start-up subsidy pays S$1.40 until 31 Dec 2028
  - its impact table also lists a stall at about S$0.67 a day
- **The pricing API** (`api/`) prices stall plans only (cooked food, fresh produce and so on), and assumes the customer pays 100%. It has no worker plan and no split by payer.
- So the todo is **not done**. The split exists only as a proposal, and nothing checks it against the API's prices.

## What Singapore already does, by payer

| Payer | Precedent in Singapore | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Platform | Platforms must buy work injury compensation insurance for platform workers and pay a CPF share, rising to employee rates by 2029 | Platform Workers Act, in force 1 Jan 2025 ([MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/platform-workers-act/cpf-contributions-for-platform-workers)) |
| Platform | Grab gives drivers free cover that includes income loss from medical leave or hospitalisation | [Grab free insurance](https://www.grab.com/sg/driver/finance/insurance/free-insurance/) |
| Government, time-limited | Platform Workers CPF Transition Support: the government pays part of lower-income platform workers' higher CPF share from 2025 to 2028, tapering each year, and stops in 2029 | [CPF Board](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/growing-your-savings/government-support/platform-workers-cpf-transition-support) |
| Union | NTUC GIFT: NTUC pays the premium (unions co-pay) for group life cover of every union member, insured by Income, with automatic enrolment | [NTUC GIFT](https://www.ntuc.org.sg/uportal/memberships/deals-for-members/ucare-gift/) |
| Worker | foodpanda riders can buy micro-insurance through SNACK at S$0.30 to S$0.70 a delivery | [pandariders.sg](https://pandariders.sg/deals/insurance/snack-by-ntuc/) |
| Stallholder | About two-thirds of cooked food stallholders buy their own stall insurance. NEA is reviewing whether to make it compulsory. | MSE written reply, 4 Aug 2026 ([link](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-insurance-for-hawkers/)) |
| Stallholder | Income Stallholder Insurance (fire and public liability): S$109.00, S$152.60 or S$218.00 a year, from S$0.30 a day, GST included, rates from 1 Jul 2024 | Income brochure, as quoted in search results. The PDF link in the brief now returns 404, so this is unchecked. |

Lessons from the brief: a worker paying alone fails (Delhi trial, KB Insurance's stallholder cover). It works when someone else carries the premium (Meituan, Jeju). Donor dependence (SEWA) is what "no permanent subsidy" rules out.

## Check against our unit economics

**Who pays doesn't change Income's economics.** The premium is set by the risk, then split between payers. Income still gets 60% claims, 25% expenses and a 15% margin, as long as the full premium arrives.

**How you sell it does change the price.** Group or embedded cover, through a platform, NTUC or a Stallholder Insurance renewal, cuts selling costs. Enrolling everyone also stops people buying just before a storm. Lower expenses alone change the price like this:

| Expense ratio | Weekly price for the same cover | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 25% (tariff today) | S$4.40 | – |
| 20% (the brief's ceiling) | S$4.13 | −6% |
| 15% (group scheme) | S$3.88 | −12% |

**After the subsidy ends on 31 Dec 2028, its S$1.40 (32% of S$4.40) has to come from somewhere:**

| Way to replace it | Worker | Platform | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026–2028, as proposed | S$1.50 | S$1.50 | S$4.40 with subsidy |
| 2029, worker and platform split it | S$2.20 | S$2.20 | S$4.40 |
| 2029, group selling (15% expenses) and a lower uncertainty load (15% to 5%) once real claims exist | S$1.79 | S$1.79 | S$3.57 |

So even in the best case, the worker's share still rises by about S$0.30 a week from 2029. The pitch should say so, and taper the subsidy each year the way the CPF transition support does, not stop it in one step.

## Two problems found in our own prices

1. **The pricing API has no worker plan.** All its products are stall goods. The S$4.40 worker plan is not priced from our data. Riders need a product with no stock loss (product factor of about 1).
2. **Tier 1 is poor value where loss days are common.** For a cooked food stall in central at Tier 1:
   - expected loss days are 8.6 a year, against a cap of 10
   - the stall pays S$312 a year (S$6.00 a week) for at most S$200 back, and S$172 expected

   That looks like paying in more than you can ever get back. It would fail fair dealing on optics alone. Options:
   - raise the Tier 1 cap
   - raise the triggers, so payouts are rarer and larger
   - make Tier 1 the default only where expected loss days are well under the cap
   - Tier 2 (S$12.01 a week, at most S$600) doesn't have this problem
