# Do Singapore insurers refuse payouts for extreme weather? (13 Nov 2026)

Notion todo (Global Doc, owner Deval): *Check Singapore "act of God" / catastrophe exclusion
clauses: do SG insurers refuse payout for extreme weather, or just price it higher? Also relevant
to the "once-in-a-blue-moon" item.* Raised by Ashutosh in the 13 Nov brainstorm.

## Short answer

Singapore insurers don't refuse payouts for extreme weather as a rule. When a policy names a
weather peril, it pays for it, and the insurer controls the cost through price, excesses,
conditions and catastrophe capital. "Act of God" does appear as a general exclusion in at least
one wording (AIG), but the same policy then **writes flood and earthquake back in as named
perils**.

The real gap is that **today's policies only pay for physical damage to property**. Heat, haze,
and rain that stops trading without damaging anything aren't named perils in the SME packages
we read. Business interruption pays only after insured property damage, and several clauses take
out exactly the losses our customers have:

| Clause | What it does to a hawker or outdoor worker | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Wind, rain, flood or dust damage to movable property **in the open or in open-sided buildings** is excluded | An open-air stall's stock and equipment aren't covered for weather | Etiqa Business Enterprise Solution (SME package), Section 1 All Risks, exclusion 3 |
| Gradually operating causes, including **change in temperature or humidity**, are excluded | Food or produce spoiled by heat isn't covered | Same, exclusion 5. MSIG SUMO Section 1 exclusion 8 ("atmospheric or climatic conditions (other than lightning)") |
| Business interruption only pays if insured property is lost or damaged by a covered cause | A day lost to heat, haze or rain with no damage pays nothing | Etiqa BES Section 2 "The Cover". MSIG SUMO Section 2 ("as a result of contingencies Covered in Section 1") |
| Denial of access and utilities extensions also need **physical damage** (to nearby property, or at a power or water station for over 6 hours) | A flooded road that blocks customers, with no damage, pays nothing | Etiqa BES Section 2, extensions 5 and 8 |
| Flood excess of **S$1,000 per loss** | Bigger than a typical day's lost income for our customers, so small flood losses pay nothing | MSIG SUMO Section 1 exclusion 22. Section 10 item 9(b) |
| Cold-room stock spoilage excludes flood "or similar natural catastrophes" | A refrigeration failure caused by a flood isn't covered | MSIG SUMO Section 8 (Deterioration of Stock), exception 1(a) |
| Pollution reaching property "through the atmosphere" is excluded unless a listed peril caused it | Haze is not an insured peril | Etiqa BES Section 1 exclusion 18 |
| Haze bought after it was publicised is a "known event", so there's no cover | Shows that insurers block anti-selection, not the event itself | Budget Direct travel (see sources). Treatment varies by insurer |

Swiss Re says the same about haze: "conventional property insurance did not cover the financial
losses resulting from a haze event", which is why it launched the parametric, PSI-triggered
HazeShield in 2018.

## Where weather *is* covered, and how it's priced

- **HDB Fire Insurance master policy** (Etiqa, V1.0 Aug 2024), clause 5.3.2, covers loss or damage
  to the flat's structure "directly caused by earthquake, hurricane, cyclone, typhoon, windstorm,
  heavy rainfall and floods howsoever caused". The extreme weather is covered, not excluded.
- **AIG Homes Essential**, General Exclusion 1(b): "loss or damage due to an act of God unless
  otherwise stated in Part B". Part B lists earthquake and flood as insured perils with no excess.
  Windstorm damage is excluded unless all doors and windows were closed. So "act of God" is used
  as a catch-all exclusion, and the insurer then buys back the named perils.
- **MSIG SUMO** (SME package, SMO022401, Jan 2024), Section 10, covers "flood howsoever caused"
  and storms, but with an excess (S$1,000 for flood, S$200 for storm) and no consequential loss.
- **Etiqa BES** clause 48, the "72-hour clause", counts all storm or flood damage within 72 hours
  as one occurrence, so the customer pays one deductible. This is a standard way to bound
  catastrophe claims without refusing them.
- **Personal accident** cover can even extend *into* weather: Etiqa BES Section 4 extension 2
  pays for death from accidental exposure to "violent and severe or prolonged weather
  conditions".
- **Motor:** most comprehensive policies cover flood damage, but not all (Singapore Legal Advice,
  Jan 2018, a secondary source). For scale, the June and July 2010 Orchard floods cost the
  industry S$22.9m in claims, S$11.6m of it motor (General Insurance Association figure, quoted
  second-hand. We haven't found the original GIA release).

### Catastrophe capital (regulator side)

On 24 Jul 2025 MAS consulted on a **General Insurance Catastrophe Risk Requirement** under RBC 2.
For the Singapore Insurance Fund, **flood is the only prescribed natural-catastrophe scenario**,
calibrated to a **1-in-200-year event over one year**. Other material risks go in an insurer's own
"Own Bespoke" scenario. (Allen & Gledhill, 28 Aug 2025; Rajah & Tann.) We haven't confirmed
whether it's final or when it starts. **Correction for Notion:** the weighted checklist cites
"MAS RBC 2 (Notice 133)" for a 1-in-200 catastrophe charge. Notice 133 is the RBC 2 base. The
catastrophe charge itself is the 2025 proposal.

Our reading, not a sourced fact: haze isn't a prescribed scenario, so Income would carry it in
its own bespoke scenario. That makes haze reinsurance and a hard yearly cap on haze payouts
matter more.

## What this means for SafeDay

1. **No "act of God" exclusion.** The weather is the insured event. Excluding it would make the
   product pointless, and it would fail MAS Fair Dealing ("honest about when it does not pay").
2. **Pitch line:** today's SME packages pay only after property damage, exclude open-air stalls
   for weather, and exclude heat spoilage. SafeDay pays on the reading itself. It sits next to
   SmartBiz 360 and doesn't replace it.
3. **Exclusions we *should* keep**, short enough for the one-SMS rule:
   - Waiting period, plus a sales freeze once NEA announces haze or a storm (the "known event"
     rule, used in travel cover).
   - War, nuclear and terrorism (standard in every wording we read, for example AIG CyberEdge
     3.7).
   - Days the customer wasn't covered or wasn't trading (insurable interest, Insurance Act s151).
   - No overlap with work-injury compensation (from the brief).
4. **Once-in-a-blue-moon events (for example the 2026 haze): pay them, but limit them.** The
   market's tools are the ones above: excess, one occurrence per 72 hours, yearly limits,
   catastrophe capital and reinsurance. For us that means a cap on payable days per season and a
   yearly limit per policy (already in the weighted checklist, section E). The catastrophe and
   reinsurance load (section F) is sized to a 1-in-200 year. An overall pool limit with pro-rata
   payouts is an option to discuss with Income. It's our idea, and we haven't found it in a
   Singapore wording.
